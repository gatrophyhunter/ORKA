# How accurate is ORKA? (validation against published data)

ORKA covers **centerfire rifle** only. These numbers are the honest answer to "is it better than GRT?". They are regenerated by `npm run data` (`scripts/calibrate-powders.ts`, then `scripts/validation-summary.ts`) and shown in the app's **Validation** tab. The numbers below come from the rifle-only build of 2026-11-23.

## Method

- **Data:** published, pressure-tested loads:
  - Hodgdon 2025 Annual Manual: Hodgdon, IMR, Winchester, Accurate and Ramshot powders; piezo psi, and CUP for some cartridges.
  - Reload Swiss 2025 guide: pressures in bar.
  - Shooters World manual.
  - Vihtavuori 2026 guide: velocities.

  Rifle scope: 24,907 published rifle loads; 23,336 had enough sourced inputs to simulate. CUP pressures are never converted to psi, so those loads contribute velocity only.
- **Held-out test (leave-one-cartridge-out):** each powder is refitted *without* one cartridge's loads and then predicts that cartridge's published velocities and pressures.
  - Only cartridges whose test-case brass capacity is published (by the brass maker or a measured comparison) are held out.
  - Training for the test uses only loads with published capacities, so no inferred quantity leaks into it.
  - The design holds out up to 6 cartridges per powder, but **in practice only .308 Win and .223 Rem qualify**. They are the only cartridges where the publisher's test brass (Winchester) has a published capacity. Every held-out number below was therefore measured on those two mid-size cartridges. For other cartridges, such as magnums, small-capacity cases and big bores, accuracy is **not measured**, and the app says so on predictions outside the validated set.
  - One known leak: the shot-start pressure of monolithic bullets (a single global value) was fitted on loads that include the held-out cartridges. Its effect on the reported errors is small, but the test is not perfectly clean.

## Results (held out)

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Pressure comparisons | 359 (48 powders) |
| Median absolute pressure error | **12.8 %** |
| 90th-percentile absolute pressure error | **31.2 %** |
| Mean signed pressure error (bias) | **−4.8 %**, i.e. the model predicts *lower* pressure on average, which is the unsafe direction |
| Predictions within ±10 % | 37 % |
| Velocity comparisons | 570 |
| Median / 90th-percentile velocity error | 71 fps / 230 fps |

In-sample (the fitted data itself) the median is 9.1 % (90th percentile 24.4 %). The powder models are fitted on rifle loads only.

## What this means

- **ORKA's pressure predictions are not accurate enough to develop loads from.** A typical prediction is off by about ±13 %, and one in ten is off by more than ±31 %. On average the model reads about 5 % low.
- GRT users report around 3 % error for well-developed powder models, per a forum post cited in `research/raw/competitors.md`. That claim could not be verified, but ORKA is clearly **not** at that level. The main reason is that GRT's powder models come from closed-vessel measurements, while ORKA's are fitted to published load data. Published data carry the publisher's test components (brass capacity, bullet bearing surface), and those are mostly unpublished.
- What ORKA **does** offer:
  - It states its error. Every prediction shows the measured model error, an upper estimate, and a sensitivity band sized from that powder's own fit.
  - It puts the maker's published loads for your components beside the prediction and flags any charge above the published maximum.
  - Every number traces to a source document.
  - Comparisons between powders in the parametric search are relative, so they are less affected by the shared biases than absolute pressures are.
- Calibrate with **your own chronograph data** (Calibrate tab) for your rifle and components. Velocity calibration narrows velocity error, but it cannot validate pressure.

## Known contributors to the error

1. **Test-case capacity.** Most publisher test cases (Winchester, Hornady, Remington brass) have no published capacity. For 8,685 of 23,336 loads, an "effective capacity" was inferred from the data itself.
2. **Bullet length.** Hornady and Sierra publish no bullet lengths, so 17,344 of 23,336 loads use an estimated length. The leave-one-out error of the estimator is 6.9 % for pointed bullets and 6.1 % for blunt (flat-nose) bullets.
3. **Model structure.** There is one shot-start pressure per construction class (fitted: monolithic 49.5 MPa vs the 25 MPa jacketed reference), no bearing-surface or jump effects, a single form function per powder, and Lagrange gradients.
4. **Burn-rate charts.** Chart-interpolated "estimated" powders have an RMS burn-rate error of 40 % when re-estimated for calibrated powders. They are labelled "not for load development".

## Data that could not be used

- **Rifle loads that could not be simulated** (out of 24,907):
  - 762 loads in cartridges without bore/groove dimensions in the standards
  - 809 loads without COAL or barrel length
  - 315 loads whose powder isn't in the database
  - 64 loads with no capacity that could be inferred

  Loads in wildcat cartridges (no SAAMI/C.I.P. standard) are outside the rifle database and are not counted here.
- **Hosts that could not be reached:**
  - Western Powders (Accurate/Ramshot product pages): connection reset. Those powders come from Hodgdon's chart and manual instead.
  - ADI: unreachable.
  - Alpha Munitions: bot checkpoint, not circumvented.
  - Hornady's catalog CDN.
  - shop.hodgdon.com.
