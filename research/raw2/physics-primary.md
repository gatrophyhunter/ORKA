## (a) IBHVG2 user's guide (BRL-TR-2829, 1987)

### (a1) Heat-loss model and default constants

Summary:

- **Model.** The heat-loss rate comes from the Black & Comenetz treatment (NDRC 1946) [a01, a02]:
  - convection from the gas, at its space-mean temperature, into a thin steel shell, by Newton's law of cooling [a06];
  - a Nordheim-Soodak-Nordheim heat-transfer coefficient (friction factor × c_p × mean gas density × mean gas velocity) plus a natural-convection term h0 [a07, a08];
  - shell temperature from its heat capacity, density, thickness and exposed area, plus a fraction HTFR of the bore-friction work [a09].
- **Errata.** The errata sheet corrects D.18 and D.21. Both corrections are illegible in the OCR [a10].
- **Default constants.** These are transcribed cleanly from ARL-TR-348 (the 1994 IBHVG2 input reference in SI) [a04]:
  - TSHL = 0.0001016 m (= 0.004 in);
  - CSHL = 460.316318 J/kg·K;
  - RSHL = 7861.0916 kg/m³;
  - TWAL = 293.0 K;
  - HO = 11.34821852 W/m²·K;
  - HL = 1 (heat loss on).
- **OCR disagreements.** The 1987 guide's English-unit OCR tokens for CSHL, RSHL and HO disagree with these values or are unreadable. Exact unit conversion shows they are consistent with 1848.0 in·lbf/lbm·K, 0.284 lbm/in³ and 0.0648 lbf/(in·s·K) [a03 conflicts].
- **Friction factor.** In IBHVG2 (D.20) the constants 13.2 and 4·log10 are legible, but the argument is not. AMCP 706-150 eq. 3-3 gives λ = (13.2 + 4·log10 d)^-2 with d in cm, and its worked example checks [b12, b13].

## (b) AMCP 706-150 (1965): Résal energy equation, Lagrange distribution, heat transfer; plus the Lagrange p(y) in Hunt and Corner

Summary:

- **Energy equation.** Fc/(γ−1) = MV²/2 + P(AX + U_ch − Cu)/(γ−1), with:
  - M = [(1+θ)W + C/3]/g;
  - θ = (2πk/nd)² + θ1 ≈ 0.01 + 0.04 = 0.05 (artillery);
  - heat loss through an adjusted γ, (γ̄−1)/(γ−1) = 1 + f, where f is about 0.15 'but may be considerably larger ... especially in small arms' [b02–b07].
- **Pressure ratios.** The handbook's breech : mean : base ratio (eq. 2-20) 'yields poor results' for small arms [b08].
- **Pressure distribution p(y).** Hunt (eqs 7,06/7,07/7,13) and Corner (eq. 13) give the quadratic Lagrange distribution between breech and base:
  - p = p_s[1 + ½(1 − σ²)·C/W], where σ is the volume fraction behind the section;
  - p_m = ⅓(2p_b + p_s);
  - p_b/p_s = 1 + C/2W;
  - p_m/p_s = 1 + C/3W;
  - with bore resistance (Corner): p(x) = p_s + (C/2W)(1 − x²/y²)(p_s − p_r) [b16, b18, b18b, b20].
- **Heat-transfer chapter.** h = 0.5·λ·C_p·ρ·v, and λ = (13.2 + 4 log10 d_cm)^-2. A caliber .50 MG gives λ ≈ 5×10⁻³ [b10–b13].

## (e) Secondary-work factor K1 and pressure exponent n

Summary:

- **No source gives a measured small-arms K1.** The published factors are all artillery-oriented:
  - AMCP 706-150: 1 + θ = 1.05, with θ1 ≈ 0.04 friction and ≈ 0.01 rotation, plus C/3 for the gas [b04];
  - Hunt: 1.05 w + C/3, made up of 1% recoil and 4% friction [b15, c02];
  - Corner: rotational m ≈ 1.005 at 30 cal/turn and 1.02 at 15 cal/turn [e04].
- **Small-arms context.** Corner says x is largest for small calibres [b23], and AMCP says the heat-loss f is larger in small arms [b05]. Both statements bear on how the losses should be split.
- **Pressure exponent n.**
  - The one small-arms value found is from Stiefel 1980 (US small-arms propellants), quoting Riefler & Lowery's closed-bomb constant-exponent model for ball propellants: **n = 0.8053**, with r in in/s and P in psi [e05]. The OCR shows 0.8033 once.
  - General gun-propellant statements: AMCP gives 0.8–0.9, and assumes 0.8 in the Taylor system [e01, b09]. Corner gives 'commonly near 0.9', with 1.02 the highest production value [e02, e03].
  - Hunt Table 5.01 gives per-propellant α for British and Dupont gun propellants [e08-*].
